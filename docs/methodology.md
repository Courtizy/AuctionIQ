# How the numbers work

[Back to the README](../README.md)

The detailed method behind AuctionIQ's prices, profits, sales estimates and reports.

## Prices, profit and sales

**Prices.** AuctionIQ reads prices through TSM, with two formulas of its own:

- *Reagent cost (aiqbuy)*: what buying it costs today, the cheapest listing but never below 80% of market value.
- *Sale value (aiqvalue)*: the lower of that and the price TSM would post it at.
- *Post price (aiqpost)*: the price AuctionIQ would post at, from the region's sale average.

To change one, add a custom source with that name in TSM (Settings, Custom Sources): AuctionIQ uses
yours instead of its formula, and your TSM operations can use the same names. The old names from
before the rename (chmat, chvalue, chpost) still work, as a TSM source or in your formulas, until you
rename them in TSM; a source under the new name wins.

When you've searched an item at the AH in the last 15 minutes, a reagent is priced from the real listings instead: buying 200 walks up the order book, so it costs what those 200 really cost, not 200 times the cheapest one. Each item in Ready to post shows the AH, TSM and Oribos Exchange prices side by side.

**Cost basis.** How reagents you already hold are counted (Settings, or `/aiq basis`):

| Basis | Reagents cost |
|---|---|
| Held (default) | What the ones you hold would sell for after the cut, and today's price for the rest |
| Replacement | Today's price to buy them all again |
| TSM | TSM's material cost, as you set it up in TSM |
| Paid | What you paid on average over the last 30 days, else today's price |

Your craft results (`/aiq results`) keep three stages, each on one basis it names. The **forecast** is on the basis you had set when you crafted. **Actual** is the market when made: items at their value, and reagents used and saved at today's price. **Realized** is what sold, less those same reagents at that same price. The hover's "forecast to realized" breakdown moves between them in steps. Moving the forecast's reagents from your basis to today's price is a step of its own ("Cost basis: held at sale value to today's price"), so it isn't hidden in "Market by craft time".

**Profit per craft.** Multicraft, Resourcefulness, Ingenuity and Concentration use the same formulas as [CraftSim](https://github.com/derfloh205/CraftSim): the extra items Multicraft gives on average, the reagents Resourcefulness hands back, and the Concentration Ingenuity refunds. Revenue is after the 5% AH cut, less the deposits you expect to lose to expired auctions and the cost of gold sitting on the AH while it sells.

**Will it sell?** Each item's chance of selling starts from the region's sale rate (TSM) and moves toward your own record as you sell and expire.

**How many a day?** Batch sizes, gold a day, posting quantities and restocks all rest on how many you can expect to sell a day. AuctionIQ works it out from your own sales, but only on the days you had the item listed, since a day with nothing up says nothing about demand. A listed day is a day you sold, expired or cancelled one, or a day AuctionIQ saw it among your auctions. While you have only a few listed days, the estimate leans on TSM's region sold a day instead. That figure is every realm in the region, so it counts at most 10 a day (`/aiq cap 20` to change). After a few weeks of listing, your own pace takes over. Wherever the number appears it says which it is ("your sales", "your sales + region" or "region") and whether the cap applied.

**How often it sells.** The chance a listing sells, behind expected proceeds, Learn and the advisor's sell rate, starts at TSM's region sale rate and moves toward your own last 30 days. Your share is by units (of the auction units that sold or expired, how many sold), but it counts once for each day with a sale or an expiry, not once per unit: the units in one stack sell or expire together, so an expired stack of 20 is one day against the item, not 20 failures, and it doesn't matter how many rows TSM splits a stack into. Vendor and trade sales don't count, since they were never listed. Hovers say what it rests on ("12 sold, 8 expired over 5 days").

**Batch results count only what you crafted.** When a batch's sales are matched, stock you already held before the craft, and stock you bought afterwards, are counted as selling first. Which unit really sold can't be known, so this is the cautious reading: a resold stack never makes a batch look better, or moves the advisor's price shading. The batch's hover says how many sales went to that other stock. Batches crafted before this change have no record of what you held, so for them only later purchases count.

**How many to make?** The Craft card's Suggested row keeps three numbers apart: how many you can make, how many you can afford, and how many AuctionIQ suggests. The suggestion is only as specific as the evidence. With a few weeks of your own sales it gives a size ("Make 12"). With thin evidence it says so and offers a small trial covering about two days of sales ("Try 4"). When your stock already covers the next few days it says "Hold off", and how many days your stock covers. Hover the row to see what it rests on, your expected week at 5, 10 and 20 (and at the quantity you set), the assumptions, and what would change the suggestion. It is optional: set any quantity with the stepper and queue it. When you queue a different number, AuctionIQ saves both. Accounting > Reports > Performance > Batch results then judges the batch against what you chose, and its hover says "You chose 20 (suggested 8)" with what the suggested size would have brought in by now (modeled), so you can see whether going your own way paid off. The Craft pane in Compare crafts uses the same suggestion and shows it beside an Overview hand-off's quantity when the two differ.

**Market trends.** Accounting > Markets > Market trends lists every priced item in your groups; Market by group rolls them up. **Trend** is a straight line through the log of the daily market price your AH scans saw over the last 30 days, per week: +10% means the line rises 10% a week. It needs 5 days scanned; until then it shows TSM's realm market against its 60-day historical price, marked "vs 60d", which says where the price is, not how fast it moves. **Volatility** is how far the price moves from one scanned day to the next (the standard deviation of daily log changes; a two-day gap counts as two days of moves), also from 5 days. **Liquidity** is units sold a day (your record blended with the region's), the region's sale rate, the gold a day that sells, and how many days what you hold and have up lasts at that rate. Each gets one word: thin (under 0.2 a day sells), choppy (moves 12% a day or more), rising or falling (5% a week or more), else steady. A group weighs its items by the gold a day they sell for. Only grouped items you've scanned, hold or have up are listed: TSM's groups can hold tens of thousands of items with nothing to chart. Selecting an item charts its daily price beside the table. The list is worked out a few milliseconds a frame when a view opens (it says so until it fills in) and kept a minute while no scan is added, so the game never stalls on it.

**Risk.** Accounting > Markets > Risk lists each item in your groups that you hold or have up. Its downside price is where the price stays above 95% of the time while what you hold sells: price x e^(-1.64 x volatility x sqrt(days)), the days being how long it takes to sell what you hold at its sales rate (1 to 30; 30 if it doesn't sell). Exposed is what you hold and have up at today's price; at risk is what it would lose at the downside. An item with too few days scanned for a volatility of its own uses the middle volatility of your items that have one, marked "typ.". Hover a row for the numbers.

**Capital allocation.** Accounting > Markets > Capital allocation ranks what you could put gold into now by how much it earns a day for each gold in: profit / gold in / days until it sells. Crafts come from your saved scans at the batch Compare crafts suggests (gold in is the batch's reagents, and it's tied up until the batch sells at its sales rate); a craft with no sales rate isn't counted. Your gold on this character, less what you keep back (10% by default; the button steps 0, 10, 25 and 50%), goes to the best return first, a whole craft at a time, and the footer says what goes in, the profit, about how much a day, and what's left idle. Hover a row for its numbers.

**Performance.** Accounting > Statements > Performance shows eight weeks of profit and what changed it against the period before. Each change is split into price, volume and cost by item, and the three add up to the item's change exactly. Click an item to open its Profit drivers on the same two periods. Expenses, lost deposits and other income open their transaction list on the same period and character, and the time frame button names that period until you pick another one. A week's bar opens that week's Income Statement. Profession, Batches and the other detail views are linked under the charts.

**By profession.** Accounting > Statements > Performance > Profession sums the time frame's crafted sales by the profession that makes each item (from your scans): units sold, revenue after the cut, what they cost to make, profit, margin and share of profit. Held is the crafted stock you hold that each profession makes, at AuctionIQ value, and days to turn is that over its revenue a day. The footer gives your inventory turnover: the cost of everything that sold (the Income Statement's) over the average of the daily inventory snapshots in the time frame, and how many days one turn takes.

**Companion app.** The addon keeps 30 days of daily prices. `companion.py`, in the `AuctionIQ_History` addon folder, keeps more: run it on your PC (Python 3, nothing to install) and it reads AuctionIQ's saved variables from every account under `WTF\Account`, adds each day to a history in `WTF\AuctionIQ_History.sqlite` (a year is kept; addon updates leave WTF alone), and writes the last 180 days to `AuctionIQ_History\History.lua`. Log in or `/reload` and Market trends charts the whole history, with its high and low and the change over 90 days; the trend and volatility still use the last 30 days. Saved variables are written when you log out or `/reload`, so run it after one, or leave `python companion.py --watch 15` running while you play. `--csv prices.csv` also writes the history as CSV, and `--wow` names the `_retail_` folder if it isn't found. An addon update replaces History.lua with an empty one until the next run.

**Overview ranking.** The Overview reads top to bottom: the gold trend, beside it gold, stock value (an estimate) and net worth (the two added up) with what changed the gold, then the Next moves. Next moves cards rank by one number: the expected gain from acting now over the next week, after the AH cut and expected deposits (shown as `+250g exp.`). Spending Concentration counts what the points add over the plain craft, not the whole craft's profit. Posting counts what Ready to post would post, at what a listing is worth to you. Restocking counts the last scan's profit for the crafts it takes. A restock card from a profession scan over 12 hours old shows no gold and says to rescan first. A restock card on screen checks the craft's options, and says so when selling your reagents or buying the item out to relist beats making it. A grouped card ("Restock 9 items") checks the first 10 of its items that have a scan, and counts the ones that need a fresh scan or have a better option by kind, such as "of the 9 items, 2 need a fresh scan first, selling the reagents earns more for 1". Past sales, reagent costs and stock values are shown on hover as evidence, labelled, but never ranked against a gain. A card with no estimate (no scan yet, say) shows no gold and ranks by urgency. Each card also says what the move rests on, in one line under its reason: the horizon its gain counts over (a 7-day gain, or "act now" and "this week" for a move with no estimate), the alternative ("or sell the reagents" when that earns more, else the usual one for that kind of move), and how old its data is (the profession scan's age, how recent your TSM sales log is, or "live"). Hover a card to see what its gain rests on, with the alternative spelled out.

**Historical costing.** How AuctionIQ books what a craft cost and what it earned:

- *Costs are booked when you craft.* Each craft saves its reagents' cost at that moment (`AuctionIQ.db.batches`), and that cost is never revalued, so a price change today changes nothing about a batch already made. Each batch notes where its cost came from. Most come from the reagents the craft used. When those weren't known, the cost is an estimate from TSM's material cost for the item. When neither was available, the cost is missing.
- *Estimated and missing costs show as such.* In Forecast accuracy (Accounting > Statements > Performance > Forecasts), a realized figure resting on an estimated cost is marked `*`, and one with no cost recorded is marked `?` and counted as 0. The footer says how many of each there are.
- *TSM sales are matched, not imported.* AuctionIQ never copies TSM's sales into its own records. Every time it needs them, it matches TSM's records to your batches again from scratch, so running it twice gives the same answer, and a sale can't be counted twice.
- *Each sale goes to at most one batch.* Within an item, sales go first to stock you held before the craft or bought later. After that they go to batches, oldest first. A unit no batch accounts for is counted as other stock.
- *Realized profit, cash flow and unsold stock are kept apart.* Realized profit is what sold less its booked cost. Unsold stock is shown on its own, at its booked cost, and never counted in realized. Cash flow (Accounting > Statements > Cash Flow) is gold in and out, whenever the reagents were bought.
- *The miss is split four ways.* A batch's difference from its forecast is split into sale price, sales pace (sold faster or slower), materials (reagents cost more or less) and yield (Multicraft made more or fewer). The four add up to the miss.

**Learning from results.** Every craft saves what was expected. Later sales are matched to it, and items that keep selling under the forecast are shaded down in the rankings.

**Presets.** Each specialization preset is the knowledge point planner run for one focus, then tested in 200 market simulations: each draws selling prices, reagent costs and sales from how much the item's price has moved over the last 30 days. Every preset gets the same draws, so "wins" is a fair head to head.

## Saved data

Everything stays on your PC in `WTF\Account\<account>\SavedVariables\AuctionIQ.lua`. AuctionIQ reads TSM's saved data but never writes to it, and sends nothing anywhere.

A TSM full scan shows AuctionIQ twenty thousand items, so cleanup runs on its own every time you log out or `/reload`. It keeps what you hold, sold in the last 30 days, craft, queue, alert on or searched yourself, and drops the rest (they come back the next time the AH shows them). It also drops a character's data for professions it no longer has. The days each item was listed are kept for 60 days, one small number per item per day.

**Clean up** (Settings, Saved data, or `/aiq cleanup`) also lets go of what AuctionIQ keeps only for speed: cached prices, auction depth, TSM's parsed records and their time order, demand, forecasts and the advisor's worked-out results. The game's collector then runs a few milliseconds a frame, and the chat line gives addon memory before and after. The figure counts every addon, and any garbage that was already waiting. TSM's records then come back in the background, so the next Overview isn't a cold one. Clean up never touches your queue, settings, groups, crafting records, decisions or history.

**Prune history** follows TSM's rule for old data (TSM Settings, Accounting, Clear Old Data). You choose the age, 365 days to start, and it lists what will go and asks before removing anything. It covers AuctionIQ's own day-by-day sales totals, net worth days and competition notes. Days you prune stay gone even while TSM still has their records. Your TSM data is left alone; clear it in TSM if you want it gone there too. Crafting records follow CraftSim's rule instead: they have no age limit and go only when the profession does. AuctionIQ keeps the newest 5,000 craft batches, so the saved file stays a safe size. `/aiq prune 365` does the same from chat.
